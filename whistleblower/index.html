Whistleblower (CEPA) · New Jersey

Blew the Whistle in New Jersey — and Paid for It?

In short — Whistleblower (CEPA) in New JerseyNew Jersey's Conscientious Employee Protection Act (CEPA) protects employees who disclose, object to, or refuse to participate in illegal or fraudulent employer activity, and it is considered the strongest whistleblower statute in the country.

CEPA requires employers to post notices about whistleblower rights and makes it unlawful to fire or retaliate against an employee who: discloses or threatens to disclose illegal activity, provides information to an investigating agency, objects to or refuses to participate in activity they reasonably believe is illegal or fraudulent, or acts in ways public policy protects.

The protected report can be internal — to a supervisor — in many circumstances. And the employee only needs a reasonable belief that a violation occurred; the employee does not have to be right about the law's fine print.

CEPA claims carry significant remedies, and CEPA cases can also trigger the New Jersey Insurance Fraud Prevention Act and False Claims Act in fraud scenarios.

Frequently Asked Questions

What protections does CEPA give NJ employees?
Protection from retaliation — firing, demotion, or other adverse action — for reporting or refusing to participate in illegal or fraudulent activity, with a private right to sue for damages.
Who counts as a whistleblower under CEPA?
Any employee who discloses, threatens to disclose, or refuses to participate in activity they reasonably believe violates law or public policy — or who assists an investigation.
How long do I have to file a CEPA claim?
Generally one year from the retaliatory act. CEPA's deadline is shorter than the NJLAD's, so whistleblower cases are urgent.
