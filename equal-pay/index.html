Being Paid Less for Comparable Work in New Jersey?

In short — Equal Pay in New JerseyThe Diane B. Allen Equal Pay Act — part of the NJLAD — prohibits paying an employee less than a colleague for substantially similar work because of any protected characteristic, and it allows recovery of up to six years of back pay plus treble damages.

New Jersey's equal pay law is deliberately broader than the federal Equal Pay Act. It covers every protected characteristic the NJLAD covers — not only sex — and it compares employees performing 'substantially similar' work, a broader standard than 'equal work.'

The six-year statute of limitations is three times the federal period, and a violation is treated as an unlawful employment practice, opening the door to compensatory and punitive damages.

Pay gaps that reflect a legitimate seniority or merit system are lawful. But gaps that track gender, age, or another protected characteristic — including those baked in by starting-salary history — violate the Act.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is 'substantially similar' work?
Work requiring similar skill, effort, and responsibility under similar conditions — a broader comparison than the federal 'equal work' standard.
Can employers ask about my salary history in NJ?
No. New Jersey bans employers from screening applicants based on prior salary, and past pay cannot justify a discriminatory wage gap.
How far back can an equal pay claim reach?
Up to six years of back pay, and willful violations can be trebled under the Wage Collection Law.
