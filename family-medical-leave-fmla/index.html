In short — Family and Medical Leave (FMLA) in New JerseyThe federal Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) and the New Jersey Family Leave Act (NJFLA) give eligible employees protected, unpaid, job-protected leave for their own or a family member's serious health condition and for bonding with a new child, with reinstatement rights when the leave ends.

The FMLA covers employers with 50+ employees and requires up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave for a serious health condition, a new child, or a family caregiver situation. The NJFLA covers private employers with 30+ employees and provides similar leave for family care and new-child bonding.

Interference with FMLA rights — refusing to approve valid leave, discouraging the employee from taking it, or counting it against attendance — is itself illegal, and terminating an employee for using protected leave is both interference and retaliation.

Employees must follow the employer's reasonable notice and certification procedures, and both laws provide up to 26 weeks for military caregiver leave under the FMLA.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I get paid during FMLA leave?
FMLA leave is unpaid, though you may use accrued paid time off, and New Jersey provides paid family and temporary disability insurance benefits that often run alongside it.
Can my employer fire me while I'm on FMLA leave?
No — not for taking the leave. Employees have the right to be reinstated to the same or an equivalent position when they return.
What if my employer has fewer than 50 employees?
The FMLA may not apply, but the NJ Family Leave Act applies at 30+ employees, and the NJLAD separately protects leave for pregnancy and disability.
