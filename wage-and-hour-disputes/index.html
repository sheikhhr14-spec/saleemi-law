Owed Unpaid Wages or Overtime in New Jersey?

In short — Wage and Hour Disputes / Unpaid Wages in New JerseyNew Jersey employees are owed one-and-a-half times their regular rate for hours over 40 per week under both state and federal law, and employers who fail to pay wages, overtime, commissions, or final paychecks on time face liquidated damages that can double the amount owed.

Wage theft takes many forms: misclassifying employees as exempt or as independent contractors, shaving hours off timesheets, averaging two workweeks to avoid overtime, forcing off-the-clock work, withholding tips, or refusing to pay out accrued wages at separation.

New Jersey's Wage Payment Law and Wage and Hour Law require timely payment and proper overtime. Violations can carry liquidated damages — effectively doubling recovery — plus penalties and attorney's fees, which is why these cases are viable even when individual amounts seem small.

Employers cannot retaliate against an employee for complaining about wages, and a recent NJ Supreme Court decision strengthened remedies for workers misclassified as independent contractors.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is overtime required in New Jersey?
Time-and-a-half for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek for non-exempt employees. Overtime is not required just for working more than 8 hours in a day.
Is my employer required to pay me overtime in NJ?
Yes, unless you fit a specific exemption — most commonly the executive, administrative, or professional exemptions. Job titles alone don't determine this; actual duties and salary do.
When must a final paycheck be paid in NJ?
Under the Wage Payment Law, wages are due on the regular payday — no special timing is required for quitting, but employees cannot be forced to wait for their pay beyond normal payroll cycles.
