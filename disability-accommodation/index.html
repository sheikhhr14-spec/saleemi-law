In short — Disability Accommodation in New JerseyUnder the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination and the federal ADA, New Jersey employers must provide reasonable accommodations — modified duties, schedule changes, equipment, or leave — that let a qualified employee with a disability perform the job, unless doing so would impose an undue hardship.

The interactive process is the heart of the law: when an employee requests an accommodation, the employer must engage in a genuine, individualized dialogue to identify a reasonable solution. Failing to engage at all is itself a violation, even if no perfect accommodation exists.

Accommodations can include modified schedules, telework where feasible, reassignment to a vacant position, assistive equipment, unpaid leave for treatment, or modification of workplace policies. The employee bears the burden of requesting — which is why a written request is so important.

The NJLAD's definition of disability is broader than the ADA's, covering many conditions the federal law might not — so New Jersey employees have stronger protection than they may realize.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a reasonable accommodation in NJ?
A change to the job, schedule, equipment, or workplace that lets a qualified employee with a disability perform essential functions — such as modified hours, remote work, assistive technology, or leave.
Does my employer have to grant the accommodation I ask for?
Not necessarily the exact one requested — but they must engage in a genuine interactive process and provide an effective accommodation if one is reasonable.
Can I be fired for requesting an accommodation?
No. Requesting one is protected activity, and firing or writing up an employee for it is retaliation under the NJLAD and ADA.
