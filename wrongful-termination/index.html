In short — Wrongful Termination in New JerseyWrongful termination in New Jersey means being fired for an illegal reason — such as discrimination, retaliation, or refusal to break the law — and employees generally have two years from the termination date to sue under the NJ Law Against Discrimination or CEPA.

New Jersey is an at-will employment state, which means an employer can generally end the relationship for almost any reason — but never for an illegal one. A termination becomes wrongful when it violates the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination (NJLAD), the Conscientious Employee Protection Act (CEPA), an employment contract, or a clear mandate of public policy.

Common red flags of a wrongful termination include being fired shortly after complaining about discrimination or unpaid wages, after reporting illegal activity, after taking medical or family leave, or as part of a pattern affecting only employees of a certain age, race, religion, or gender.

Time limits matter. Claims under the NJLAD and CEPA generally must be filed within two years, and contractual claims may have even shorter windows under their own terms. Evidence — emails, performance reviews, schedules, witness names — is easiest to preserve early.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as wrongful termination in New Jersey?
A termination is wrongful if it is based on a protected characteristic, retaliates against protected activity (like reporting discrimination or wage violations), violates an employment contract, or violates public policy.
How long do I have to file a wrongful termination claim in NJ?
Generally two years from the date of termination for NJLAD and CEPA claims. Some claims, such as breach of an employment contract, may have different deadlines, so act promptly.
Can I be fired for no reason at all in New Jersey?
Yes — NJ is an at-will state, so a termination with no stated reason is not automatically illegal. It becomes illegal if the real motivation violates a statute, contract, or public policy.
