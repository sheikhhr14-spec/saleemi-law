Should You Sign the Severance Agreement Your Employer Offered You?

Direct answer below — written by Sleemi Law, a New Jersey employment law firm in Paramus, NJ.

Ask About Your Case
Short answerNot before an attorney reviews it. A severance agreement is a contract in which you receive money in exchange for waiving your right to sue. Once signed, the waiver usually extinguishes discrimination, wage, and whistleblower claims. Employees 40 and over must receive at least 21 days to consider and 7 days to revoke a release of age discrimination claims.

The offer is rarely about generosity — the release of claims is the entire point. The employer is buying certainty. Whether the price is fair depends on what claims you are giving up, and that is exactly what a review determines.

Look past the dollar figure. Confidentiality, non-disparagement, cooperation clauses, clawbacks of bonuses, non-compete provisions buried in the appendix, and statements about your 'voluntary resignation' can all cost more than the severance is worth.

Federal law (the OWBPA) gives employees 40 and over at least 21 days to consider and a 7-day revocation period for age claims. For group terminations, the employer must also disclose the decisional unit and the ages of affected employees.

Many offers are negotiable — especially where a potential claim exists. A well-supported counter sometimes raises the payment, extends benefits, or removes an overreaching non-compete. A review is cheap; a signed release of a strong claim is not.

Related Questions

Do I have to sign a severance agreement?
No. It is voluntary — but the consideration is also voluntary. You can decline, though usually without keeping the offer.
How long do I have to decide on a severance offer?
Whatever the deadline in the agreement says — but employees 40+ get at least 21 days plus a 7-day revocation right for age claims by federal law.
Can an attorney negotiate my severance in NJ?
Yes, and it often pays for itself. Where a viable claim or overreaching terms exist, counters frequently improve the package.
