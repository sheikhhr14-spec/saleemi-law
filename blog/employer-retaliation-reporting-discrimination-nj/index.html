Can My Employer Retaliate Against Me for Reporting Discrimination in New Jersey?

Direct answer below — written by Sleemi Law, a New Jersey employment law firm in Paramus, NJ.

Ask About Your Case
Short answerNo. Under the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination, it is illegal for an employer to retaliate against an employee for reporting discrimination, participating in an investigation, or requesting an accommodation. Retaliation is a separate violation even if the underlying discrimination claim is not proven — and timing is the key evidence.

Retaliation claims often succeed where the underlying discrimination claim fails. The law protects the act of reporting; you need only a reasonable, good-faith belief that a violation occurred. If the employer punishes you for the report itself, that is a new violation.

Adverse action extends beyond firing: demotion, worse hours, removal from projects, sudden write-ups after a clean record, exclusion from meetings, or a transfer to a dead-end role can all qualify.

The evidence in most retaliation cases is temporal. Complaint on Monday, termination on Friday — with a stated reason that shifted between the meeting and the paperwork — is the classic pattern.

If you've reported discrimination and are now feeling the temperature drop at work, document everything contemporaneously and get advice early. Don't wait to be terminated to find out where you stand.

Related Questions

What is retaliation under the NJLAD?
Any adverse employment action taken because you engaged in protected activity — complaining about discrimination, cooperating in an investigation, or requesting an accommodation.
Do I need to prove discrimination actually occurred?
No. You need a reasonable, good-faith belief that it did. Punishing the report is the violation.
How long do I have to file a retaliation claim?
Two years under the NJLAD; one year for CEPA whistleblower retaliation claims.
