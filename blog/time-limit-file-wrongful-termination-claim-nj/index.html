How Long Do I Have to File a Wrongful Termination Claim in New Jersey?

Direct answer below — written by Sleemi Law, a New Jersey employment law firm in Paramus, NJ.

Ask About Your Case
Short answerMost wrongful termination claims in New Jersey — including NJLAD discrimination claims and CEPA whistleblower claims — must be filed within two years of the termination. CEPA whistleblower claims move faster than people expect, and some contract claims have shorter deadlines written into the agreement.

Deadlines decide more employment cases than juries do. In New Jersey, claims under the Law Against Discrimination (NJLAD) carry a two-year statute of limitations from the wrongful act. CEPA whistleblower claims also carry one year — shorter than most employees assume, and the reason 'I was still deciding' sinks otherwise strong claims.

Contract claims follow their own rules. A written employment agreement may require disputes to be raised within a specific window, or route them to arbitration. Severance agreements typically set their own short deadlines for revocation and rescission challenges.

Administrative routes can matter too: the NJ Division on Civil Rights and the EEOC have their own filing windows (180 or 300 days for the EEOC, 180 days for the DCR), which are needed for certain federal claims and optional for state ones.

The safest move is a consultation immediately after termination — even if you are not sure you want to sue. It preserves the timeline, identifies which deadlines apply to your facts, and costs you nothing at our firm.

Related Questions

What is the statute of limitations for wrongful termination in NJ?
Two years for NJLAD discrimination and most retaliation claims. CEPA whistleblower claims carry a one-year deadline.
Do I have to file with an agency before suing?
For NJLAD claims in state court, no. Federal Title VII claims do require an EEOC charge within 300 days.
Can a deadline be extended?
Rarely, and only in narrow circumstances. Assume the deadline is firm and act accordingly.
