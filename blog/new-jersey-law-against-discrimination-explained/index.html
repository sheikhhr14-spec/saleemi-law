What Is the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination (NJLAD)?

Direct answer below — written by Sleemi Law, a New Jersey employment law firm in Paramus, NJ.

Ask About Your Case
Short answerThe New Jersey Law Against Discrimination (NJLAD) is New Jersey's principal anti-discrimination statute. It prohibits discrimination based on race, religion, sex, gender identity, pregnancy, sexual orientation, age, disability, national origin, and other protected characteristics — and it applies to nearly all NJ employers, with no cap on compensatory or punitive damages.

Enacted in 1945, the NJLAD is one of the oldest and strongest civil rights statutes in the country. It protects more categories than federal Title VII — including gender identity or expression, breastfeeding, domestic partnership status, and genetic information — and it covers employers of nearly any size.

The statute reaches every stage of employment: hiring, pay, promotion, discipline, leave, and termination. It also bans harassment based on any protected category and retaliation for complaining about discrimination or requesting an accommodation.

Two features make the NJLAD uniquely powerful for employees: there is no administrative prerequisite to sue in state court (no EEOC charge required), and there is no statutory cap on compensatory or punitive damages, unlike federal law's damage caps.

If you believe you've been discriminated against in New Jersey, the NJLAD's two-year statute of limitations applies — and early documentation of the pattern dramatically improves outcomes.

Related Questions

Who does the NJLAD cover?
Nearly all New Jersey employers, employees, and applicants, regardless of company size — broader than federal anti-discrimination law.
What are the NJLAD's protected categories?
Race, creed, color, national origin, ancestry, nationality, sex, gender identity or expression, pregnancy, breastfeeding, sexual orientation, marital and partnership status, religion, age, disability, genetic information, and military service.
What can I recover under the NJLAD?
Back pay, front pay, emotional distress damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees — with no cap on compensatory or punitive damages.
