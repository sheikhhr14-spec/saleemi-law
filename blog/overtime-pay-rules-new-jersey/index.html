Is My Employer Required to Pay Me Overtime in New Jersey?

Direct answer below — written by Sleemi Law, a New Jersey employment law firm in Paramus, NJ.

Ask About Your Case
Short answerYes — New Jersey employees who work more than 40 hours in a workweek must be paid one-and-a-half times their regular rate of pay, unless they fall under a specific exemption such as the executive, administrative, or professional exemptions. Paying a salary does not automatically exempt an employee from overtime.

Both the New Jersey Wage and Hour Law and the federal Fair Labor Standards Act require overtime after 40 hours per workweek. New Jersey does not require daily overtime — the trigger is the 40-hour week, not the 8-hour day.

The most common violation we see is misclassification: an employee given a manager title and a salary who spends most of the week doing non-exempt work. Exempt status depends on actual duties and a salary basis — not a title, and not the fact that you are paid a salary.

Other frequent violations include off-the-clock work (opening, closing, prep, or training time), automatic lunch deductions that don't match reality, averaging hours across two weeks, and misclassifying employees as independent contractors to sidestep overtime entirely.

Remedies are strong: liquidated damages can double the unpaid amount, and employers who retaliate against employees for complaining about wages face separate liability under the NJLAD.

Related Questions

Is overtime required after 8 hours in a day in NJ?
No. Overtime is required after 40 hours in a workweek, not after 8 hours in a day.
If I'm paid a salary, do I still get overtime?
Usually yes, unless your actual duties fit a specific exemption. A salary alone does not make you exempt.
How do I calculate what I'm owed?
Unpaid overtime equals the number of overtime hours times one-half your regular rate, then doubled for liquidated damages in successful claims.
