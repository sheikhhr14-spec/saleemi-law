Pregnant and Not Getting Workplace Accommodations in New Jersey?

In short — Pregnancy Accommodation in New JerseyThe New Jersey Pregnant Worker Fairness Act and the NJLAD require employers to provide reasonable accommodations — lighter duty, more frequent breaks, schedule changes, or modified equipment — for pregnancy, childbirth, and related medical conditions, and prohibit forcing an employee onto leave when an accommodation would let her keep working.

New Jersey's Pregnant Worker Fairness Act, in effect since 2024, treats pregnancy like any other condition requiring accommodation: the employer must engage in the interactive process and provide a reasonable accommodation unless it would impose an undue hardship.

Accommodations can include modified schedules, rest breaks, assistance with manual labor, temporary transfer to less strenuous roles, remote work, or leave. Employers may not force an employee to take paid or unpaid leave when a reasonable accommodation would allow her to continue working.

The NJLAD separately prohibits discrimination because of pregnancy, breastfeeding, or the intent to become pregnant — including failure to hire and firing shortly after a pregnancy announcement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What accommodations does pregnancy entitle me to in NJ?
Reasonable ones: schedule changes, breaks, lighter duty, modified equipment, temporary transfer, or leave, unless the employer shows undue hardship.
Can my employer force me to take leave while pregnant?
No. If a reasonable accommodation would let you keep working, the employer cannot compel you to take leave instead.
Does the NJLAD protect breastfeeding at work?
Yes — the NJLAD protects breastfeeding and requires reasonable accommodation for it, including break time and a private space that is not a bathroom.
