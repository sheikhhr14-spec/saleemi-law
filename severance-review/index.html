In short — Severance Review / Negotiation in New JerseyA severance agreement in New Jersey is a contract: the employee receives money or benefits and, in exchange, signs away the right to sue the employer — so every severance offer should be reviewed by an attorney before signing, especially the release of claims and any non-disparagement or cooperation clauses.

Employers rarely offer severance out of pure generosity. A release of claims is the point of the payment — and once signed, it typically ends nearly every claim you might have had, from discrimination to unpaid wages.

Employees 40 and over receive special protections under the federal OWBPA, which requires specific language, disclosure of other affected employees in group terminations, and a 21-day consideration period plus a 7-day revocation right for age claims.

A severance offer is often an opening number. Claims that are strong, evidence that is damaging, or deadlines the employer has missed all create leverage to negotiate more money, better references, continued benefits, or removal of restrictive covenants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I sign the severance agreement my employer offered me?
Not before an attorney reviews it. Signing usually waives your right to sue in exchange for the payment, and the offer is often negotiable.
Can I negotiate my severance in New Jersey?
Yes. Many offers increase with a well-supported counter, particularly where a potential claim exists or the release terms overreach.
How long do I have to decide?
For employees 40 and over, federal law guarantees at least 21 days to consider a release of age claims plus a 7-day revocation window. Other cases depend on the deadline the employer sets — never let a short fuse pressure you.
