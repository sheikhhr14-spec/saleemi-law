In short — Retaliation in New JerseyRetaliation in New Jersey is any adverse action — termination, demotion, schedule or pay cuts, exclusion, or hostile treatment — taken because an employee engaged in protected activity such as complaining about discrimination or reporting illegal conduct, and it is illegal under the NJLAD and CEPA.

Retaliation law protects the act of speaking up. An employee does not have to win the underlying complaint — a good-faith report of discrimination, wage violations, or safety problems is protected even if the investigation finds nothing.

Retaliation is usually proven with timing. A termination a week after a written complaint, a sudden negative performance review after a wage claim, or hours cut after someone agrees to be a witness — these patterns tell the story.

Adverse action is broader than firing. Demotion, transfer to a worse position, exclusion from meetings, sudden write-ups after years of good reviews, and other materially negative changes can all qualify.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is protected activity under NJ law?
Complaining about discrimination or harassment, requesting an accommodation, reporting wage violations or illegal conduct, refusing to participate in illegal activity, and filing a workers' compensation claim.
Do I have to prove my original complaint was correct?
No. You only need a good-faith, reasonable belief that a violation occurred. Retaliation is about the punishment, not the outcome of the complaint.
How do I prove retaliation?
Mostly through timing and pattern: protected activity followed closely by adverse treatment, combined with shifting explanations from the employer.
