Religious Accommodation · New Jersey

Denied a Religious Accommodation at Work in New Jersey?

In short — Religious Accommodation in New JerseyNew Jersey employers must reasonably accommodate an employee's sincerely held religious beliefs and practices — including scheduling around Sabbath or holiday observance, prayer breaks, and religious dress or grooming — unless the accommodation would cause an undue hardship on business operations.

The NJLAD requires employers to accommodate religious observance — shift swaps, schedule adjustments, uniform exceptions for religious garb or grooming — when the request can be met without undue hardship. New Jersey's standard is at least as protective as federal Title VII.

An accommodation request does not require magic words or theological proof. The employee signals the conflict between a work rule and a religious practice; the employer must then explore reasonable solutions rather than simply refusing.

Retaliating against an employee for requesting a religious accommodation is independently unlawful, as is disciplining an employee for practicing the accommodated observance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What religious practices must be accommodated in NJ?
Sincerely held beliefs and observances — worship, holidays, Sabbath, dress, and grooming practices — must be accommodated when reasonable.
What is an undue hardship for religious accommodation?
A real, substantial cost or disruption to business operations — not a supervisor's inconvenience or customer preference.
Can my employer discipline me for religious dress?
Not if the dress is part of a sincerely held practice and a reasonable exception is available. Refusing to even consider one violates the NJLAD.
