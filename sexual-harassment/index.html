In short — Sexual Harassment in New JerseySexual harassment in New Jersey is any unwelcome sexual advance, request for sexual favors, or verbal or physical sexual conduct that affects employment decisions or creates a hostile work environment, and it is illegal under the NJLAD and Title VII.

Harassment takes two main legal forms. Quid pro quo harassment ties a job benefit — hiring, promotion, continued employment — to submission to sexual demands. Hostile work environment harassment makes the workplace intolerable through repeated sexual comments, jokes, images, texts, or touching.

The harasser can be a supervisor, a coworker, a vendor, or a customer, and the victim can be any gender. What matters is that the conduct is unwelcome and that it is severe or pervasive enough to alter working conditions.

Employers are liable when supervisors engage in harassment, and when they knew or should have known about coworker or third-party harassment and failed to take prompt, effective corrective action.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if I'm being sexually harassed at work?
Tell the harasser to stop if you safely can, preserve all texts, emails, and messages, report it in writing to HR or management, and contact an employment lawyer — you don't have to quit before getting advice.
Can I be fired for reporting sexual harassment?
No. Retaliation for a good-faith harassment complaint is independently illegal under the NJLAD, even if the underlying complaint can't be proven.
Is one incident enough for a harassment claim?
A single severe incident — such as assault — can be enough. Otherwise the law looks at whether the conduct is pervasive, meaning repeated and cumulative.
